## Summary: Compensation Equity Audit

### Key Findings

**1. Gender Pay Gap in Base Salary (CONCERN)**
- **Unadjusted gap**: Females earn $16,000 less annually than males ($127,500 vs $143,500), representing an 11.2% disparity
- **Most of this gap disappears when role-adjusted**: 
  - When controlling for **level only**: gap shrinks to 1.5%
  - When controlling for **function only**: gap is 10.5% (suggesting function distribution matters more)
  - When controlling for **both level and function**: gap further reduces to just **0.5%**

**Interpretation**: The bulk of the gender pay gap appears to be driven by compositional differences—females are underrepresented in higher-paying roles/levels rather than being systematically underpaid within the same roles. The residual 0.5% gap after full adjustment is negligible.

**2. Bonus Allocation (INFO)**
- No gender gap exists: all groups receive an average of 12% bonus
- This suggests equitable bonus practices across genders

**3. Equity Compensation (INFO)**
- **Unadjusted gap**: Females receive $5,500 less in annual equity value (4.1% less)
- **After role control**: Females actually receive **3% MORE equity** than males in equivalent roles
- This suggests equity is allocated fairly or even slightly favorably to women when role-adjusted

### What Was Refused and Why

**Highest Individual Salary**: This request was **refused** by the system's policy design, not by explicit human override. The disclosure policy excludes statistics like "min", "max", and "top-N", only permitting "count" and "mean" aggregations. This is a deliberate privacy protection: extreme values are most likely to identify individuals. The numeric floor of 20 prevents small-cohort averages from being revealed, but individual extremes are protected from the start.
